Why the predictor uses rules: the degradation study

The first idea was to tailor strategy to each driver's tyre wear. The data below is why that was dropped.

No stable per-driver or per-team trend

The gaps between drivers (about 0.01 to 0.02 s/lap) are far smaller than the stint-to-stint scatter (about 0.09 s/lap), and a driver's figure from odd rounds does not predict the figure from even rounds. Degradation is only reliable as one field-wide value per race and compound.

What this means

  • A per-driver or per-car wear rate should not go into the strategy model. Adding one inside a single race made the projected order worse in the backtest.
  • Lap-time slope measures how the driver chose to run the stint. A car that is hard on tyres often shows a flat slope because the driver backs off.
  • The projected order beats the running order in dry races, by 12% on the races used for tuning and 4% on held-out races. Most of the gain is mid-race.
  • Wet races and the first quarter of any race are not reliable yet.
  • Car differences that may be measurable instead: base pace, and how long each team's stints last before pitting.
